## 1. Adonai

- **Scripture reference:** Psalm 8:1
- **Verified exact verse text (WEB Classic, eBible.org — VERIFIED 2026-06-30):** "Yahweh, our Lord, how majestic is your name in all the earth! You have set your glory above the heavens!" — Source: https://ebible.org/eng-web/PSA008.htm
- **Difference from prior draft:** the draft ran this as one continuous clause ("...in all the earth, who has set your glory above the heavens!"); the verified text is two separate sentences ("...in all the earth! You have set your glory above the heavens!"). Use the verified two-sentence form going forward.
- **Short definition (garment/card use):** "Lord; a title of reverence and authority."
- **Longer devotional explanation (draft):** Adonai is a name used throughout scripture to address God as sovereign Lord and Master — it's a posture of submission and trust, not just a label. Wearing or carrying the name is meant as a quiet daily reminder of who holds authority over a life, not a claim about God's full nature.
- **Theological nuance / wording risk to flag:** WEB renders the Hebrew divine name (YHWH) as "Yahweh" rather than the more familiar "LORD," and Psalm 8:1 actually contains both — "Yahweh, our Lord [Adonai]." This is technically accurate and arguably stronger for an "Adonai" card (the word itself appears in the verse), but it will look unfamiliar to readers used to "O LORD, our Lord" phrasing from other translations. Confirm you're comfortable with "Yahweh" appearing on this card/garment, since it's a more literal but less common English rendering.
- **Lane:** Premium/GV Archive (already locked)
- **Proposed front/back/card usage:** Front — "ADONAI" wordmark only (name stays primary). Back — short definition + Psalm 8:1 in archive-label type, using the confirmed verse text once approved. Study card — definition + longer devotional line + verse, with the nuance note above available as an optional "context" expansion.

## 2. El Shaddai

- **Scripture reference:** Genesis 17:1
- **Verified exact verse text (WEB Classic, eBible.org — VERIFIED 2026-06-30):** "When Abram was ninety-nine years old, Yahweh appeared to Abram and said to him, "I am God Almighty. Walk before me and be blameless."" — Source: https://ebible.org/eng-web/GEN17.htm
- **Difference from prior draft:** punctuation only — the draft had a comma after "him" and a comma before "and be blameless"; the verified text has neither comma. No wording change.
- **Short definition (garment/card use):** "God Almighty."
- **Longer devotional explanation (draft):** El Shaddai is the name God uses when He establishes His covenant with Abram — a declaration of complete sufficiency and power at the exact moment Abram is asked to walk in obedience. It pairs strength with relationship: not a distant power, but one who is "enough" for whatever is asked.
- **Theological nuance / wording risk to flag:** the Hebrew "El Shaddai" itself does not appear in the WEB English text — WEB translates it directly as "God Almighty," same as most major translations. This means the garment's headline name ("EL SHADDAI") is a transliteration choice you're making for the brand, while the verse text underneath will read "God Almighty," not "El Shaddai." Confirm you're fine with the headline and the quoted verse using two different forms of the same name — this is normal in study/devotional branding but worth a conscious yes.
- **Lane:** Premium/GV Archive (already locked)
- **Proposed front/back/card usage:** Front — raised "EL SHADDAI" lettering (primary concept). Back — minimal archive text: definition only ("God Almighty"), with the full Genesis 17:1 line reserved for the study card rather than crowding the hoodie back. Study card — definition + longer devotional line + verse + the nuance note as context.

## 4. Hope

- **Scripture reference:** Romans 15:13
- **Verified exact verse text (WEB Classic, eBible.org — VERIFIED 2026-06-30):** "Now may the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, that you may abound in hope in the power of the Holy Spirit." — Source: https://ebible.org/eng-web/ROM15.htm
- **Difference from prior draft — flagged, this one matters:** the verified official text reads "Now **may** the God of hope fill you..." — the draft was missing "may." The verified text also drops the comma the draft had before "in the power of the Holy Spirit." Use the verified wording ("Now may the God of hope...") going forward, not the earlier draft.
- **Short definition (garment/card use):** "Confident expectation rooted in God's faithfulness."
- **Longer devotional explanation (draft):** Hope here isn't wishful thinking — it's expectation grounded in who God has already shown Himself to be. The verse frames hope as something that grows ("abound") rather than something you either have or don't, which fits well with a wearable, ongoing "study" framing rather than a one-time slogan.
- **Theological nuance / wording risk to flag:** WEB's phrasing "fill you with all joy and peace in believing" is grammatically a touch archaic-sounding for casual readers ("fill" rather than "may fill," subjunctive mood retained from the Greek) — it reads accurately but slightly more formal than the brand's generally modern voice. Worth deciding whether to quote it exactly as-is (recommended, since it's the actual translation) or trim to a shorter clause for the garment graphic while keeping the full verse on the study card.
- **Lane:** Living Water/Urban (already locked)
- **Proposed front/back/card usage:** Front — "HOPE" wordmark, expressive front chest statement. Back — a trimmed phrase from Romans 15:13 (or the full verse, pending your call above) as a streetwear-style back graphic lockup, with the Living Water logo placed per the locked Lane 1 system. Study card — definition + longer devotional line + full verse text.
